Sketch the general shape of six root loci

Sketch the general shape of the root locus for each of the open-loop pole-zero plots (a) to (f).

Nise, chapter 8, problem 2, p. 433. Section 8.4. The figure gives no numbers, so the exact positions below are read from it (for example poles at −1 and −2); the shapes do not depend on the exact values.

The sketching rules (section 8.4) and why they hold

RuleWhat it saysWhy
BranchesThere are as many branches as closed-loop poles: \(n\), the number of open-loop poles (when \(n\ge m\)).\(1+KG(s)H(s)=0\) is a polynomial of degree \(n\).
SymmetryThe locus is symmetric about the real axis.Complex roots of a real polynomial come in conjugate pairs.
Real axisFor \(K>0\), a real-axis point is on the locus if it lies to the left of an odd number of real open-loop poles plus zeros.Angle condition: every real pole or zero to the right adds 180°; complex pairs cancel.
Start and endBranches start at the open-loop poles (\(K=0\)) and end at the open-loop zeros (\(K\to\infty\)), finite or at infinity.\(D(s)+KN(s)=0\): \(K=0\) leaves \(D=0\); large \(K\) forces \(N\approx0\).
Asymptotes\(n-m\) branches go to infinity along lines from \(\sigma_a=\frac{\sum p-\sum z}{n-m}\) at angles \(\theta_a=\frac{(2k+1)\,180^\circ}{n-m}\).Far away, \(G(s)\approx1/s^{n-m}\) seen from the centroid.

Every point \(s\) on the locus satisfies the angle condition \(\angle G(s)H(s)=(2k+1)\,180^\circ\), and the gain that puts a pole there is \(K=1/|G(s)H(s)|\). Hover over the s-plane to test any point.

Sketch the root locus of three unity-feedback systems

Sketch the root locus of the unity-feedback system for

\[\text{(a)}\;G(s)=\frac{K(s+2)(s+6)}{s^2+8s+25},\qquad\text{(b)}\;G(s)=\frac{K(s^2+4)}{s^2+1},\qquad\text{(c)}\;G(s)=\frac{K(s^2+1)}{s^2}\]

Nise, chapter 8, problem 3 (a, b, c), p. 433. Section 8.4.

Walkthrough

Zeros: \(s=-2\) and \(s=-6\). Poles from the quadratic formula:

\[s=\frac{-8\pm\sqrt{8^2-4\cdot25}}{2}=\frac{-8\pm\sqrt{-36}}{2}=-4\pm j3\]

Only \([-6,-2]\) has an odd count (the zero at \(-2\)) to its right.

\(n=m=2\): both branches end on the zeros \(-2\) and \(-6\).

At the pole \(-4+j3\), angles from the zeros: \(\angle(-2+3j)=123.7^\circ\), \(\angle(2+3j)=56.3^\circ\); from the other pole: \(\angle(6j)=90^\circ\).

\[\theta_d=180^\circ+123.7^\circ+56.3^\circ-90^\circ=270^\circ\equiv-90^\circ\]

The upper branch leaves straight down.

This one can be solved exactly. \(1+G=0\) gives

\[(1+K)s^2+8(1+K)s+(25+12K)=0\;\Rightarrow\;s=-4\pm j\sqrt{\frac{9-4K}{1+K}}\]

The real part is always \(-4\): the branches run straight down the line \(\sigma=-4\) and meet at the break-in point \(s=-4\) when \(K=9/4=2.25\). Check: \(D'N-DN'=(2s+8)(12-25)=0\) at \(s=-4\).

For \(K>2.25\) the two poles split along the real axis toward \(-2\) and \(-6\). The closed loop is stable for every \(K>0\).

\(s^2+4=0\Rightarrow\) zeros at \(\pm j2\). \(s^2+1=0\Rightarrow\) poles at \(\pm j\).

No real poles or zeros, so nothing on the real axis.

\(n=m=2\): the branches end on the zeros \(\pm j2\).

\[\theta_d=180^\circ+\underbrace{(-90^\circ)}_{\text{zero }+j2}+\underbrace{90^\circ}_{\text{zero }-j2}-\underbrace{90^\circ}_{\text{pole }-j}=90^\circ\]

The upper branch leaves straight up, along the \(j\omega\) axis.

\[(1+K)s^2+(1+4K)=0\;\Rightarrow\;s=\pm j\sqrt{\frac{1+4K}{1+K}}\]

The poles climb the \(j\omega\) axis from \(\pm j\) (\(K=0\)) to \(\pm j2\) (\(K\to\infty\)).

Marginally stable for every \(K\): a sustained oscillation between 1 and 2 rad/s.

\(s^2=0\Rightarrow\) a double pole at the origin. \(s^2+1=0\Rightarrow\) zeros at \(\pm j\).

Left of the origin there are two poles to the right, an even count. No real-axis segment: the branches must leave the axis immediately.

\(n=m=2\): the branches end on \(\pm j\).

Two branches starting from the same point split symmetrically, here at \(\pm90^\circ\): the origin acts as a breakaway point at \(K=0\). They arrive at \(\pm j\) vertically:

\[\theta_a=180^\circ+\underbrace{2\cdot90^\circ}_{\text{double pole}}-\underbrace{90^\circ}_{\text{zero }-j}=270^\circ\equiv-90^\circ\]
\[(1+K)s^2+K=0\;\Rightarrow\;s=\pm j\sqrt{\frac{K}{1+K}}\]

The branches go up and down the \(j\omega\) axis from \(0\) to \(\pm j\).

Marginally stable for every \(K>0\).

Sketch the locus and find the break-in and breakaway points

Sketch the root locus of the unity-feedback system with \(G(s)=\dfrac{K(s+3)(s+5)}{(s+1)(s-7)}\) and find the break-in and breakaway points.

Nise, chapter 8, problem 7, p. 433. Section 8.5.

Walkthrough

Poles at \(-1\) and \(+7\): the open loop is unstable. Zeros at \(-3\) and \(-5\). \(n=m=2\).

\[KG(s)=\frac{K(s^2+8s+15)}{s^2-6s-7}\]

\([-1,7]\): one pole (\(+7\)) to the right. \([-5,-3]\): three to the right. So the poles \(-1\) and \(+7\) move toward each other, must break away somewhere between them, and come back to the axis between the zeros.

On the locus \(KG=-1\). On the real axis \(s=\sigma\), so

\[K(\sigma)=-\frac{\sigma^2-6\sigma-7}{\sigma^2+8\sigma+15}\]

Break points are where \(K\) is extreme along the axis. With the quotient rule:

\[\frac{dK}{d\sigma}=-\frac{(2\sigma-6)(\sigma^2+8\sigma+15)-(\sigma^2-6\sigma-7)(2\sigma+8)}{(\sigma^2+8\sigma+15)^2}=-\frac{2(7\sigma^2+22\sigma-17)}{(\sigma^2+8\sigma+15)^2}\]
\[7\sigma^2+22\sigma-17=0\;\Rightarrow\;\sigma=\frac{-22\pm\sqrt{22^2+4\cdot7\cdot17}}{14}=\frac{-22\pm30.98}{14}\]
\[\sigma_1=0.642,\qquad \sigma_2=-3.78\]

Skip the derivative: on the real axis the break points satisfy \(\sum\frac{1}{\sigma+z_i}=\sum\frac{1}{\sigma+p_i}\), with \(z_i,p_i\) the negatives of the zero and pole values:

\[\frac{1}{\sigma+3}+\frac{1}{\sigma+5}=\frac{1}{\sigma+1}+\frac{1}{\sigma-7}\]
\[(2\sigma+8)(\sigma^2-6\sigma-7)=(2\sigma-6)(\sigma^2+8\sigma+15)\;\Rightarrow\;14\sigma^2+44\sigma-34=0\]

The same quadratic (times 2), the same two roots.

On \([-1,7]\), \(K\) rises from 0 and falls back to 0: \(\sigma=0.642\) is a maximum of \(K\) (\(K=0.508\)), the breakaway point. On \([-5,-3]\), \(K\) comes down from infinity and goes back up: \(\sigma=-3.78\) is a minimum (\(K=31.5\)), the break-in point. The K(σ) plot below shows both.

The characteristic polynomial is \((1+K)s^2+(8K-6)s+(15K-7)\). A second-order polynomial is stable when all its coefficients are positive, so the closed loop is stable for \(K>0.75\). At \(K=0.75\):

\[1.75s^2+4.25=0\;\Rightarrow\;s=\pm j1.558\]

The complex part of this locus is a circle through the two break points: centre \(-1.571\), radius \(2.213\). The marker at the origin is the pole that started at \(-1\): it moves right and enters the right half-plane at \(K=0.467\) (where \(15K-7=0\)), before meeting the other pole at the breakaway point. Feedback with \(K>0.75\) stabilises an open-loop unstable plant.

Result
\[\text{breakaway } \sigma=0.642\ (K=0.508),\qquad \text{break-in } \sigma=-3.78\ (K=31.5),\qquad \text{stable for }K>0.75\]

Lab: K along the real axis

The curve is \(K(\sigma)=-D(\sigma)/N(\sigma)\), drawn only where the real axis is part of the locus. The amber line is the current gain from the slider above: wherever it meets the curve there is a real closed-loop pole. When the line rises above the breakaway maximum it no longer meets that segment, because those two poles have become complex.

Sketch the locus and find the range of K for stability

Sketch the root locus and find the range of \(K\) for stability of the unity-feedback system with

\[\text{(a)}\;G(s)=\frac{K(s^2+1)}{(s-1)(s+2)(s+3)},\qquad\text{(b)}\;G(s)=\frac{K(s^2-2s+2)}{s(s+1)(s+2)}\]

Nise, chapter 8, problem 14, p. 434. Section 8.5.

Walkthrough

Poles at \(+1\), \(-2\), \(-3\) (one in the right half-plane); zeros at \(\pm j\). \(n=3\), \(m=2\).

Only real poles and zeros count; the zeros \(\pm j\) do not. On \([-2,1]\) there is one pole to the right; left of \(-3\) there are three. Both are on the locus.

\(n-m=1\): one asymptote at \(180^\circ\). The centroid formula gives \(\sigma_a=\frac{(1-2-3)-(0)}{1}=-4\), but with a single asymptote at \(180^\circ\) it is simply the negative real axis, which the branch from \(-3\) already follows.

The poles \(+1\) and \(-2\) approach each other on \([-2,1]\) and break away toward the zeros \(\pm j\). The transition method must include all zeros, complex ones too:

\[\frac{1}{\sigma-1}+\frac{1}{\sigma+2}+\frac{1}{\sigma+3}=\frac{1}{\sigma-j}+\frac{1}{\sigma+j}=\frac{2\sigma}{\sigma^2+1}\]
\[(3\sigma^2+8\sigma+1)(\sigma^2+1)=2\sigma(\sigma^3+4\sigma^2+\sigma-6)\;\Rightarrow\;\sigma^4+2\sigma^2+20\sigma+1=0\]

Real roots: \(\sigma=-0.050\) (on \([-2,1]\), \(K=6.03\): the breakaway) and \(\sigma=-2.449\) (between \(-3\) and \(-2\), not on the locus). Arrival angle at \(+j\): \(180^\circ+(135^\circ+26.6^\circ+18.4^\circ)-90^\circ=270^\circ\), so the branches reach the zeros vertically from below.

Note on the posted solution

Case (a), breakaway point. The posted solution keeps only the real poles, \(\frac{1}{s-1}+\frac{1}{s+2}+\frac{1}{s+3}=0\), and a stray factor 5 appears in the expansion. The zeros at \(\pm j\) must be included, which gives \(\sigma^4+2\sigma^2+20\sigma+1=0\) and a breakaway at \(\sigma\approx-0.050\), not \(-0.13\). In the K(σ) plot the posted value is marked in coral: it is not where \(K\) peaks.

Case (a), Routh table. The entries \(1\) and \(K-6\) are swapped between the \(s^3\) and \(s^2\) rows; the correct rows are \([1,\;1]\) and \([4+K,\;K-6]\). The conclusion \(K>6\) is still right.

Case (b). The range should read \(0<K<0.791\): the \(s^0\) entry \(2K\) also has to be positive.
