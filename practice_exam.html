Question 1

15 marks

The figure presents the block diagram of a system. \(T(s)\) represents the closed-loop transfer function,

\[T(s)=\frac{Y(s)}{R(s)}=\frac{s^{2}+(K+5)s+10}{s^{4}+2Ks^{2}+1}.\]

From Midterm 2025, Question 1.

1.1Find the range of values of \(K\) that makes the system \(T(s)\) unstable, using the Routh–Hurwitz criterion. [7 marks]

1.1aComplete Routh array of the characteristic polynomial4 marks

Characteristic polynomial: \(D(s)=s^4+0\,s^3+2Ks^2+0\,s+1\). The \(s^3\) row is entirely zero, so use the auxiliary polynomial \(P(s)=s^4+2Ks^2+1\), with \(P'(s)=4s^3+4Ks\).

\[\begin{array}{c|ccc} s^4 & 1 & 2K & 1\\ s^3 & 0\to \mathbf{4} & 0\to \mathbf{4K} & \\ s^2 & K & 1 & \\ s^1 & \dfrac{4(K^2-1)}{K} & & \\ s^0 & 1 & & \end{array}\qquad \begin{aligned} b_1&=\tfrac{4(2K)-1(4K)}{4}=K,\quad b_2=\tfrac{4(1)-1(0)}{4}=1\\ c_1&=\tfrac{K(4K)-4(1)}{K}=\tfrac{4(K^2-1)}{K}\end{aligned}\]
How marks are awarded1 — polynomial/first row and recognising the zero \(s^3\) row; 1 — auxiliary polynomial and its derivative; 2 — rows \(s^2\), \(s^1\), \(s^0\) correct (1 each for \(s^2\) and \(s^1\)).
1.1bRange of \(K\) for which \(T(s)\) is unstable, with justification3 marks

Because the \(s^3\) row vanishes, \(D(s)\) is even: its roots are symmetric about both axes, so the system can never be asymptotically stable.

  • \(K \gt 1\): no sign changes below the zero row ⇒ four distinct roots on the \(j\omega\)-axis, \(s=\pm j\sqrt{K\mp\sqrt{K^2-1}}\) ⇒ marginally stable.
  • \(K=1\): the \(s^1\) row vanishes again ⇒ repeated roots \(s=\pm j\) (double) ⇒ unstable.
  • \(0 \lt K \lt 1\): \(c_1 \lt 0\) ⇒ two sign changes ⇒ two RHP poles; \(K\le0\): also two sign changes (or \(s^4+1=0\) for \(K=0\)) ⇒ unstable.
\[\boxed{\text{Unstable for } K\le 1}\qquad(\text{marginally stable for } K \gt 1)\]
How marks are awarded2 — correct range (\(K \lt 1\) alone without discussing \(K=1\): 1 of these 2); 1 — justification by sign changes / marginal stability for \(K \gt 1\).

1.2Sketch the pole-zero diagram of the closed-loop system for \(K=2\). Clearly indicate the exact values and positions of both poles and zeros on the complex plane. Use “×” for poles and “○” for zeros. [3 marks]

1.2aValues of the zeros and poles for \(K=2\)2 marks

Zeros: \(s^2+7s+10=(s+2)(s+5)=0\;\Rightarrow\;\boxed{z_1=-2,\;z_2=-5}\)

Poles: \(s^4+4s^2+1=0\;\Rightarrow\;s^2=-2\pm\sqrt3\;\Rightarrow\;\boxed{p_{1,2}=\pm j\sqrt{2-\sqrt3}=\pm j0.518,\quad p_{3,4}=\pm j\sqrt{2+\sqrt3}=\pm j1.932}\)

How marks are awarded1 — zeros; 1 — poles.
1.2bPole-zero map (× poles, ○ zeros) — sketch on paper1 mark
How marks are awarded1 — all four poles on the imaginary axis and both zeros on the negative real axis, correct symbols.

1.3Based on the pole and zero locations, indicate and justify the expected system behaviour (overdamped, underdamped, critically damped or marginally stable) for \(K=2\). [2 marks]

1.3Expected behaviour and justification2 marks

Marginally stable. All four poles are simple (distinct) and lie on the imaginary axis (\(\sigma=0\), \(\zeta=0\)), so there is no damping: the response contains sustained oscillations at \(\omega_1=0.518\) and \(\omega_2=1.932\) rad/s that neither decay nor grow. The LHP zeros at −2 and −5 only change the amplitudes and phases of these modes, not stability.

How marks are awarded1 — “marginally stable”; 1 — justification (simple poles on the \(j\omega\)-axis, no real part, undamped oscillation).

1.4A controller \(C(s)\) is introduced so that the closed-loop step response has approximately 9.5 % overshoot and a settling time \(T_s=\tfrac56\) s (2 % criterion). Determine the desired second-order characteristic equation, clearly indicating \(\zeta\) and \(\omega_n\). [3 marks]

1.4aDamping ratio \(\zeta\)1 mark
\[\zeta=\frac{-\ln(0.095)}{\sqrt{\pi^2+\ln^2(0.095)}}=\frac{2.354}{3.926}\approx\boxed{0.60}\]
1.4bNatural frequency \(\omega_n\)1 mark
\[\omega_n=\frac{4}{\zeta T_s}=\frac{4}{0.6\cdot 5/6}=\boxed{8\ \text{rad/s}}\]
1.4cDesired characteristic equation1 mark
\[s^2+2\zeta\omega_ns+\omega_n^2=\boxed{s^2+9.6s+64=0}\qquad(\text{poles } s=-4.8\pm j6.4)\]
How marks are awardedAllow follow-through from 1.4a/1.4b.

Worked solution

The characteristic equation is \(D(s)=s^4+2Ks^2+1=0\). The coefficients of \(s^3\) and \(s^1\) are zero, so the whole \(s^3\) row of the Routh array is zero (special case). Replace it with the coefficients of \(dP/ds\), where \(P(s)=s^4+2Ks^2+1\) is the auxiliary polynomial formed from the \(s^4\) row. The first column becomes \(\{1,\;4,\;K,\;4(K^2-1)/K,\;1\}\).

A row of zeros means \(D(s)\) has roots placed symmetrically about the origin; here \(D(s)=P(s)\), so all its roots are symmetric. With \(\lambda=s^2\): \(\lambda^2+2K\lambda+1=0\Rightarrow\lambda=-K\pm\sqrt{K^2-1}\).

  • \(K \gt 1\): both \(\lambda\) real and negative ⇒ four distinct imaginary roots ⇒ marginally stable.
  • \(K=1\): \(\lambda=-1\) double ⇒ \(s=\pm j\) repeated: modes \(t\sin t\) grow ⇒ unstable.
  • \(|K| \lt 1\): \(\lambda\) complex ⇒ roots in all four quadrants ⇒ unstable.
  • \(K\le-1\): \(\lambda\) real and positive ⇒ a real root in the RHP ⇒ unstable.

So \(T(s)\) is unstable for \(K\le1\), marginally stable for \(K \gt 1\), and never asymptotically stable. The plot shows how the roots move with \(K\).

\(N(s)=s^2+7s+10=(s+2)(s+5)\) and \(D(s)=s^4+4s^2+1\), so \(\lambda=-2\pm\sqrt3=\{-0.268,\,-3.732\}\), giving \(s=\pm j0.5176\) and \(s=\pm j1.9319\).

The four poles are simple and purely imaginary; each pair produces an undamped sinusoid. The step response is bounded but oscillates indefinitely around the static value \(T(0)=10\): marginally stable (\(\zeta=0\)), none of the over/under/critically damped categories applies.

From \(\%OS=9.5\%\): \(\ln(0.095)=-2.354\), \(\zeta=2.354/\sqrt{\pi^2+2.354^2}=0.600\). From \(T_s=4/(\zeta\omega_n)\): \(\omega_n=4/(0.6\times0.8\overline{3})=8\) rad/s. Thus \(2\zeta\omega_n=9.6\), \(\omega_n^2=64\):

\[s^2+9.6s+64=0,\qquad \omega_d=\omega_n\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}=6.4\ \text{rad/s}\]

Check: \(\%OS=100e^{-0.6\pi/0.8}=9.48\%\), \(T_s=4/4.8=0.833\) s.

2.1Find the transfer function \(G(s)=V_L(s)/V_s(s)\) for the circuit. [6 marks]

2.1aCircuit equations in the \(s\)-domain (impedances, KCL/KVL)3 marks

Impedances: \(Z_C=\dfrac{1}{Cs}\), \(Z_L=Ls\). Let \(V_A(s)\) be the voltage of the top node of \(C\).

\[\text{KCL at }A:\quad \frac{V_s-V_A}{R_1}=Cs\,V_A+\frac{V_A}{R_2+Ls}\qquad\qquad \text{divider: }\quad V_L=\frac{Ls}{R_2+Ls}\,V_A\]
How marks are awarded1 — impedances; 1 — KCL (or equivalent mesh equations); 1 — divider / second equation. Any correct equivalent formulation earns full marks.
2.1bTransfer function \(G(s)=V_L(s)/V_s(s)\) in terms of \(R_1,R_2,L,C\)3 marks

From KCL: \(V_A\big[(R_2+Ls)(1+R_1Cs)+R_1\big]=(R_2+Ls)V_s\), hence

\[\boxed{G(s)=\frac{V_L(s)}{V_s(s)}=\frac{L\,s}{R_1LC\,s^2+(L+R_1R_2C)\,s+R_1+R_2}}\]
How marks are awarded2 — correct algebraic reduction; 1 — final expression in standard polynomial form.

2.5Sketch of \(i_L(t)\) with \(T_p\), \(T_r\), \(\%OS\) and final value — on paper2 marks

Worked solution

With zero initial conditions the capacitor and inductor become impedances \(1/(Cs)\) and \(Ls\). The \(R_2\)–\(L\) branch is in parallel with \(C\) at node \(A\). KCL at \(A\):

\[\frac{V_s-V_A}{R_1}=CsV_A+\frac{V_A}{R_2+Ls}\;\Rightarrow\;V_A=\frac{R_2+Ls}{(R_2+Ls)(1+R_1Cs)+R_1}V_s\]
\[V_L=\frac{Ls}{R_2+Ls}V_A\;\Rightarrow\;G(s)=\frac{Ls}{(R_2+Ls)(1+R_1Cs)+R_1}=\frac{Ls}{R_1LCs^2+(L+R_1R_2C)s+(R_1+R_2)}\]

With \(R_1=2\), \(R_2=1\), \(C=1\), \(L=1\): \(G(s)=s/(2s^2+3s+3)\). For \(v_s=5u(t)\): \(V_L(s)=G(s)\cdot5/s=2.5/(s^2+1.5s+1.5)\). Completing the square, \((s+0.75)^2+(\sqrt{15}/4)^2\), and with \(\mathcal L\{e^{at}\sin\omega t\}=\omega/((s-a)^2+\omega^2)\):

\[v_L(t)=2.582\,e^{-0.75t}\sin(0.968t)\ \text{V}\]

Checks: \(v_L(0)=0\), and \(v_L(\infty)=0\) because the inductor is a short circuit in DC steady state (final value theorem: \(\lim_{s\to0}sV_L(s)=0\)).

Comparing \(s^2+1.5s+1.5\) with \(s^2+2\zeta\omega_ns+\omega_n^2\): \(\omega_n=1.2247\) rad/s, \(\zeta=0.75/1.2247=0.6124\) — underdamped. \(T(s)\) has the same denominator, so

\[T_s=\frac{4}{0.75}=5.33\text{ s},\quad \%OS=100e^{-2.433}=8.77\%,\quad T_r=\frac{2.197}{1.2247}=1.79\text{ s},\quad T_p=\frac{\pi}{0.9682}=3.24\text{ s}\]

The rise-time formula is an empirical fit; the exact 10–90 % rise time of this response is \(1.94-0.41=1.54\) s.

For a unit step, \(i_L(\infty)=\lim_{s\to0}sT(s)\tfrac1s=0.5/1.5=1/3\) A, and the peak is \(\tfrac13(1+0.0877)=0.363\) A at \(t=T_p\). The exact response is

\[i_L(t)=\tfrac13\big[1-e^{-0.75t}\big(\cos0.968t+0.775\sin0.968t\big)\big]\ \text{A}\]

Consider the circuit in the figure, where \(v_o(t)\) is the voltage across the resistor and \(v_i(t)\) is the input voltage. The component values are \(L_1=L_2=1\) H, \(C=1\) F and \(R=2\ \Omega\).

Part b

3.3Sketch the poles and zeros in the \(s\)-plane and discuss the stability of the system. [4 marks]

3.3aPole-zero map with values (× poles, ○ zeros) — on paper2 marks
How marks are awarded1 — poles −3, +1; 1 — zero −3.5 (correct symbols and positions).
3.3bStability of the system and justification2 marks

Worked solution

The states are the energy-storage variables: the two inductor currents and the capacitor voltage. KCL at the node joining \(L_1\), \(R\) and \(L_2\) gives the resistor current \(i_R=x_1-x_2\), hence \(v_o=R(x_1-x_2)\).

\[\text{left mesh: } v_i=L_1\dot x_1+R(x_1-x_2),\qquad \text{right mesh: } R(x_1-x_2)=L_2\dot x_2+x_3,\qquad C\dot x_3=x_2\]
\[\dot x_1=-\tfrac{R}{L_1}x_1+\tfrac{R}{L_1}x_2+\tfrac{1}{L_1}u,\quad \dot x_2=\tfrac{R}{L_2}x_1-\tfrac{R}{L_2}x_2-\tfrac1{L_2}x_3,\quad \dot x_3=\tfrac1Cx_2,\quad y=Rx_1-Rx_2\]
\[A=\begin{bmatrix}-2&2&0\\2&-2&-1\\0&1&0\end{bmatrix},\quad B=\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\\0\end{bmatrix},\quad C=\begin{bmatrix}2&-2&0\end{bmatrix},\quad D=0\]

For a 2×2 matrix, swap the diagonal, negate the off-diagonal and divide by the determinant:

\[\operatorname{adj}\begin{bmatrix}s&-1\\-3&s+2\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}s+2&1\\3&s\end{bmatrix},\qquad \det=s(s+2)-3=s^2+2s-3\]

\(B=[1\;0]^T\) picks the first column: \((sI-A)^{-1}B=\frac{1}{\Delta}[s+2,\;3]^T\). Then

\[C(sI-A)^{-1}B=\frac{2(s+2)+3}{\Delta}=\frac{2s+7}{(s+3)(s-1)}\]

Zero at −3.5; poles (eigenvalues of \(A\)) at −3 and +1. One pole in the RHP ⇒ unstable.

The block diagram represents a satellite attitude control system with both angular-position and angular-velocity feedback. The constant gains \(K\) and \(K_v\) are to be determined.

From Final 2025, Question 2.

4.1Simplify the block diagram and determine the transfer function \(G(s)\) between \(r(t)\) and \(\theta(t)\). [10 marks]

4.1aBlock-diagram reduction: forward path and equivalent feedback path5 marks

Forward path \(e\to\theta\): \(G_f(s)=K\cdot\dfrac2s\cdot\dfrac1s=\dfrac{2K}{s^2}\).

Feedback: \(\dot\theta=s\,\theta\), so the signal returned to the comparator is \(\theta+K_v\dot\theta=(1+K_vs)\,\theta\): \(H(s)=1+K_vs\) (pick-off point moved to the output; parallel blocks summed).

\[\frac{\Theta(s)}{R(s)}=\frac{G_f}{1+G_fH}\]
How marks are awarded2 — forward path; 2 — equivalent feedback \(1+K_vs\); 1 — closed-loop formula. The inner-velocity-loop-first route (\(\frac{2K/s}{1+2KK_v/s}\), then the outer unity loop) earns full marks.
4.1bClosed-loop transfer function \(G(s)=\Theta(s)/R(s)\)5 marks
\[G(s)=\frac{2K/s^2}{1+\dfrac{2K(1+K_vs)}{s^2}}=\boxed{\frac{2K}{s^2+2KK_v\,s+2K}}\]
How marks are awarded3 — correct algebra; 2 — final simplified expression (standard second-order form).

4.2Compute the steady-state error \(e_{ss}\) of the closed-loop system for a unit-step input. [5 marks]

4.2aExpression for \(E(s)\) with \(R(s)=1/s\)3 marks
\[E(s)=\frac{R(s)}{1+G_f(s)H(s)}=\frac{s^2}{s^2+2KK_vs+2K}\cdot\frac1s=\frac{s}{s^2+2KK_vs+2K}\]
How marks are awarded2 — error transfer function; 1 — substitution of \(R(s)=1/s\). Defining \(e=r-\theta\), \(E(s)=R(s)[1-G(s)]\), is also accepted.
4.2bSteady-state error \(e_{ss}\) (state the condition for validity)2 marks
\[e_{ss}=\lim_{s\to0}sE(s)=\lim_{s\to0}\frac{s^2}{s^2+2KK_vs+2K}=\boxed{0}\]

Valid when the closed loop is stable (\(K \gt 0\), \(K_v \gt 0\)). The open loop \(2K(1+K_vs)/s^2\) is type 2, so \(K_p\to\infty\).

How marks are awarded1 — \(e_{ss}=0\); 1 — use of the final value theorem with the stability condition (or the type-2 argument).

Worked solution

The satellite is the cascade \(\frac2s\cdot\frac1s\), with \(\dot\theta\) available between the two integrators. The comparator receives \(\theta+K_v\dot\theta\).

Since \(\dot\Theta(s)=s\Theta(s)\), the velocity pick-off can be moved to the output by multiplying its feedback block by \(s\). The two parallel feedback paths then add: \(H(s)=1+K_vs\). The forward path is \(G_f(s)=2K/s^2\).

\[G(s)=\frac{G_f}{1+G_fH}=\frac{2K}{s^2+2K(1+K_vs)}=\frac{2K}{s^2+2KK_vs+2K}\]

Comparing with \(s^2+2\zeta\omega_ns+\omega_n^2\): \(\omega_n=\sqrt{2K}\) and \(\zeta=K_v\sqrt{K/2}\) — \(K\) sets the speed, \(K_v\) the damping. The plot uses \(K=2\), \(K_v=0.5\) as an example.

Alternative route: split the comparator into two summing junctions. The inner loop (\(K\cdot\frac2s\) with feedback \(K_v\)) gives \(\frac{2K}{s+2KK_v}\); cascading with \(\frac1s\) and closing the outer unity loop gives the same result.

The actuating error is \(E(s)=R(s)-(1+K_vs)\Theta(s)=\frac{R(s)}{1+G_fH}\). For \(R(s)=1/s\): \(E(s)=\frac{s}{s^2+2KK_vs+2K}\).

The closed loop is stable for \(K \gt 0\), \(K_v \gt 0\) (all coefficients of a second-order polynomial positive), so the final value theorem applies: \(e_{ss}=\lim_{s\to0}sE(s)=0\). Equivalently \(\theta_{ss}=G(0)=1\), so \(r-\theta\to0\) as well.

Lab: tune K and Kᵥ

\(K\) moves the poles away from the origin (faster); \(K_v\) adds damping. Whatever stable pair you choose, the step error goes to zero.

6.2bPhase margin (gain-crossover frequency and value)3 marks

Worked solution

\(L(s)=\frac{80\cdot1}{4\cdot20}\cdot\frac{1+s}{s(1+s/4)(1+s/20)}\) gives \(K_B=1\): the constant contributes 0 dB and 0°. The integrator alone is a −20 dB/dec line through 0 dB at \(\omega=1\) (+20 dB at 0.1) with a constant −90°.

\((1+j\omega)\): 0 dB up to \(\omega=1\), then +20 dB/dec — the sum goes flat at 0 dB. Phase: \(0\to+90^\circ\) between 0.1 and 10 rad/s.

\(1/(1+j\omega/4)\): −20 dB/dec from \(\omega=4\), bringing the sum back to −20 dB/dec. Phase: \(0\to-90^\circ\) between 0.4 and 40 rad/s.

\(1/(1+j\omega/20)\): another −20 dB/dec from \(\omega=20\) (sum −40 dB/dec). Phase: \(0\to-90^\circ\) between 2 and 200 rad/s. Asymptotic values: 0 dB at \(\omega=4\), \(-20\log_{10}5=-14.0\) dB at \(\omega=20\), −82 dB at \(\omega=1000\).

The phase is −90° (integrator) plus one lead and two lags; the net lag never reaches −180° at a finite frequency, so there is no phase crossover and \(GM=\infty\). The exact gain crossover is \(\omega_c=1.97\) rad/s, where \(\angle L=-58.8^\circ\), so \(PM=121.2^\circ\). From the asymptotic plot the magnitude is ≈ 0 dB on \(1\le\omega\le4\), which gives a PM between ≈ 110° (at \(\omega=4\)) and ≈ 121°; such readings are accepted if shown on the plot.
